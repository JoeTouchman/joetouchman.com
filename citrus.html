Software Scouting

At every competition the team records what each robot does in each match, then uses that data to plan matches and choose alliance partners. Software Scouting is the subteam that builds the six apps and the server that make this work. Front-end developers write the apps in Kotlin.

  • Kotlin
  • Jetpack Compose
  • Android Studio
  • Kotlin Multiplatform
  • Git + GitHub
  • Code review

The full system is documented in the team's 2026 Software Scouting whitepaper ↗

My main project

Match Collection

The tablet app scouts in the stands use to record everything one robot does in a match. I designed and built the 2026 Objective Match Collection app, including the interface design. Every qualification match, 18 scouts use it at once, three per robot.

  • Field map: scouts follow the robot zone to zone, and can only move to zones that connect
  • Press-and-hold shot timing with a live timer, used by the server to match shots to the scoreboard
  • Undo and redo, an incapacitated mode, and a flipped layout for the other side of the field
  • Data leaves the tablet as a QR code, since Wi-Fi isn't allowed at events and Bluetooth is unreliable

Features in team apps

Viewer: multi-team graphs

Viewer is the app the drive team and strategists read data in. I built multi-team graphs: put several teams on one chart, or split them into a scrolling list of charts.

"You made the multiple team graphs? That's the feature I used the most."Richard McCann, 1678 mentor

Viewer: team comparison

I built the comparison page, which lines up any data point for several teams side by side and opens a graph of it with one tap.

In 2026 Viewer was rewritten from Android-only to Kotlin Multiplatform, so it also runs on desktop and the web.

Stand Strategist: DNP and marks

I added "Do Not Pick" flags and match marks. A strategist can flag a team for the rest of the event, or mark one team in one match to review later. Both collect into sheets.

Teaching and outreach

Summer camp drones

I ran the drone section of the summer robotics camp on my own, with 14 drones in one classroom. I chose which drones to buy, wrote the slides, safety rules and lesson plans, and taught kids to code them through rings and onto targets. The teacher kept my materials for later camps.

Weekly robotics class

For one school year each at Davis Waldorf and Davis School for Independent Study, I was the robotics instructor for kids aged 8 to 14: building, coding and driving competition robots. I wrote my own lessons on levers, counterweights and weight distribution. The image is one of my slides.

Tournaments and outreach

At youth VEX IQ tournaments I volunteered as emcee, queuer, referee and field reset. I also ran a demo table at the farmers market.
